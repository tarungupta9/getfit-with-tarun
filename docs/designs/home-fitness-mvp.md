# Design: Explained Home Fitness Planner MVP

- Generated by `/office-hours` on 2026-08-18
- Branch: `main`
- Repo: local `getfit-with-tarun` repository

Mode: Builder

## Problem Statement

Busy adults who are new to exercise can find thousands of routines, videos, and fitness recommendations, but they still struggle to turn two simple constraints - time per session and days available per week - into one safe, coherent routine they understand well enough to follow.

The MVP serves apparently healthy adults aged 18–64 who want to begin general home fitness without equipment. It replaces repeated searching across unrelated sources with a four-week bodyweight plan that explains why each scheduling, exercise, recovery, and progression choice fits the user's commitment.

## What Makes This Useful

The product does not claim that generated fitness content is the innovation. Existing tools already offer plans, PDFs, exercise videos, and personalized schedules. The useful moment is when a beginner sees a complete weekly routine and can answer three questions immediately:

1. What do I do first?
2. Why does this plan fit the time I actually have?
3. How does it progress without requiring me to judge conflicting fitness advice?

The plan stays inspectable. It uses a small reviewed exercise catalog, deterministic rules, minimal illustrations, and direct links to manually reviewed demonstrations. It never performs an open-ended video search or free-form plan generation at runtime.

## Evidence and Product Position

- The [WHO physical activity guidelines](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK566046/) recommend regular activity, at least two days of muscle-strengthening work for adults, gradual increases in frequency, intensity, and duration, and emphasize that some activity is better than none.
- The [U.S. Physical Activity Guidelines](https://odphp.health.gov/our-work/nutrition-physical-activity/physical-activity-guidelines/current-guidelines/top-10-things-know) support the same broad direction and provide the public-health baseline behind the educational copy.
- [ACSM preparticipation screening guidance](https://journals.lww.com/acsm-msse/fulltext/2015/11000/updating_acsm_s_recommendations_for_exercise.28.aspx) warns against unnecessary barriers while still distinguishing symptoms, current activity, and intended intensity. The MVP therefore uses a visible acknowledgment and clear limitations rather than a medical diagnosis questionnaire.
- The [American Heart Association's 2026 review of AI-assisted workouts](https://www.heart.org/en/news/2026/01/05/whats-the-best-way-to-use-ai-in-your-workout) notes that software cannot observe a beginner's form or reliably handle every health scenario. The product must state that limitation beside the plan, not bury it in terms of service.

## Constraints and Boundaries

- Exercise only. Nutrition, calorie estimates, macros, meal plans, and dietary advice are excluded.
- General fitness and consistency only. Muscle building, athletic performance, CrossFit, rehabilitation, and specialized conditioning are later product areas.
- Bodyweight plus ordinary household support only: clear floor space, a wall, and a stable chair. No bands, dumbbells, benches, pull-up bars, or configurable equipment inventory.
- No account, server-side user profile, workout logging, streaks, reminders, social features, chat, or cross-device history.
- No runtime large language model and no external knowledge base in v1. Reviewed content and plan rules ship with the application. A versioned knowledge base is a later extension behind the same content and rules interfaces.
- The application is educational and cannot observe movement quality, diagnose conditions, prescribe rehabilitation, or replace a trainer or clinician.

## Premises

1. The problem is fragmented decision-making, not a shortage of exercise content.
2. A narrow, deterministic planner is more trustworthy for a beginner MVP than free-form generation.
3. A complete four-week plan is the smallest output that can demonstrate scheduling, recovery, progression, and education together.
4. Users outside the supported health and age boundary should not receive a nominally personalized plan from v1.

## Approaches Considered

- **Handcrafted template matcher:** rejected because prewriting every duration, frequency, and starting-point combination duplicates content and makes personalization shallow.
- **Curated deterministic plan compiler:** selected because it creates real schedule variation while keeping every rule and content item reviewable and testable.
- **Educational base-plan workbook:** rejected because it shifts plan adaptation back to the beginner and weakens the tailored-plan promise.

## Recommended Approach

### User journey

1. **Commitment intake:** choose 15, 30, or 45 minutes per session; choose exactly 2, 3, or 4 available weekdays; and select one starting point using observable definitions:
   - `new`: no structured exercise more than once weekly during the last three months;
   - `lightly_active`: walking, recreation, or exercise 1–3 times weekly, but no consistent full-body program during the last three months;
   - `returning`: previously completed structured exercise at least twice weekly, then stopped for 8 weeks to 12 months, with no current exercise-limiting injury.
2. **Safety acknowledgment:** show one concise disclaimer explaining the supported audience, the application's limitations, relevant warning situations, and when to seek qualified help. The user must acknowledge it before generation, but the application collects no health answers and does not persist the acknowledgment.
3. **Plan result:** receive a four-week calendar with two alternating full-body sessions, non-consecutive recovery where the chosen frequency permits, warm-ups, cool-downs, exercise dosage, and a single progression variable per week.
4. **Explanation:** show a visible reason for the frequency, duration allocation, exercise level, recovery spacing, and progression choice. Each reason traces back to a user input or a reviewed rule.
5. **Exercise detail:** show a minimal illustration, concise setup and movement cues, a regression or easier alternative, stop guidance, and a manually reviewed external demonstration.
6. **Exit:** let the user change inputs or use browser print/save-to-PDF. Persist only commitment preferences in `localStorage`; never persist a generated plan or the safety acknowledgment.

The approved rough interaction sketch is stored at [assets/home-fitness-mvp-wireframe.png](./assets/home-fitness-mvp-wireframe.png). It establishes information hierarchy only; it is not a visual design specification.

### Safety disclaimer

The v1 disclaimer is a draft content contract. A qualified fitness professional must approve its exact wording before public launch. It states that the planner provides general educational guidance for apparently healthy adults aged 18–64, cannot assess health or movement quality, identifies warning situations that warrant qualified guidance, and tells users to stop and seek appropriate care if acutely unwell.

The intake uses one required checkbox: “I understand this is general guidance and not medical advice.” The acknowledgment is UI state only. It is never written to `localStorage`, analytics, or another service.

### Plan compiler

Implement the compiler as a pure deterministic domain module with no browser or framework dependencies. It accepts a versioned intake object and returns a complete plan plus an explanation trace.

The planner input contract contains:

- `minutesPerSession`: `15 | 30 | 45`
- `availableDays`: exactly 2, 3, or 4 unique weekday values, sorted Monday through Sunday
- `startingPoint`: `new | lightly_active | returning`

Calendar placement uses the selected days exactly. Session letters alternate in chronological order. Across week boundaries, the next week starts with the opposite letter when needed to avoid repeating the same session twice in sequence.

The exercise catalog contains reviewed records with:

- stable ID and display name;
- movement family, difficulty, and compatible starting points;
- setup requirements limited to floor, wall, or stable chair;
- default repetitions or timed duration and rest;
- `selectionPriority` for deterministic tie-breaking and reviewer-approved `secondsPerRep` for duration calculation;
- nullable easier and harder exercise IDs, plus a terminal same-exercise modification for the easiest supported level;
- short form cues, stop guidance, and educational rationale;
- local illustration asset reference;
- demonstration URL, publisher, reviewer, evidence/source URL, and last-checked date.

The initial catalog stays deliberately small. Its draft seed set is below; the qualified fitness reviewer may replace an item before launch but must preserve movement coverage and the supported-equipment boundary.

| Family | Seed exercise | Easier option | Harder option |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knee-dominant | Chair squat | Sit-to-stand with hand support | Bodyweight squat to chair target |
| Posterior chain | Glute bridge | Short-range glute bridge | Paused glute bridge |
| Hip pattern | Wall hip-hinge drill | Reduced-range wall hinge | Unsupported bodyweight hinge |
| Push | Wall push-up | More upright wall push-up | Knee push-up on floor |
| Upper back/posture | Prone W raise | Standing wall W | Prone reverse snow angel |
| Trunk | Dead bug | Heel taps | Extended dead bug |
| Trunk/quadruped | Bird dog | Single-limb reach | Paused full bird dog |
| Conditioning | March in place | Slower supported march | Low-impact step jack |

Warm-up and cool-down records are separate reviewed sequences, not general exercise candidates. Every generated reference must resolve to a reviewed catalog item. CI reports unused catalog entries, but the compiler never forces an exercise into a plan merely to achieve catalog coverage.

Each exercise has a reviewer-approved four-step `PrescriptionTrack` for each compatible starting point. A step contains exact sets, repetitions or work seconds, tempo enum, range enum, and rest seconds. The compiler does not invent or arithmetically extrapolate a prescription. This keeps progression testable and makes professional review the source of truth.

Minimum main-work family coverage is normative:

| Duration | Session A required | Session B required | Optional if budget remains |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 min | Knee-dominant, push, trunk | Posterior/hip, upper-back/posture, trunk | None |
| 30 min | Knee-dominant, push, trunk, conditioning | Posterior/hip, upper-back/posture, trunk, conditioning | One opposite-emphasis family |
| 45 min | Knee-dominant, push, trunk, posterior/hip, conditioning | Posterior/hip, upper-back/posture, trunk, knee-dominant, conditioning | One additional compatible exercise |

The 15-minute A and B seed fixtures must pass the real duration validator after professional approval and before UI implementation starts. If either fails, the content/policy pair must change; the compiler must not omit a required family.

Generation rules must:

- treat displayed duration as an estimate and fit warm-up, main work, rest, transitions, cool-down, and a buffer inside the selected budget using this policy:

  | Selected duration | Warm-up | Main-work budget | Cool-down | Buffer |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | 15 min | 2 min | 10 min | 2 min | 1 min |
  | 30 min | 4 min | 21 min | 3 min | 2 min |
  | 45 min | 5 min | 33 min | 4 min | 3 min |

- compute duration canonically at build and runtime: rep-based work is `sets × reps × secondsPerRep`; timed work is `sets × workSeconds`; add rest only between sets and add one 15-second transition per exercise. No stored total-duration field may override this calculation;
- use two alternating sessions so repeated days do not become random workouts;
- alternate A/B in the user's selected weekday order; adjacent selected days are allowed, but A and B use different emphasis families and never repeat the same catalog item on adjacent days;
- evaluate balanced movement coverage across the week, not inside each 15-minute session. A emphasizes knee-dominant, push, and trunk work; B emphasizes posterior-chain, upper-back/posture, trunk, and low-impact conditioning. Longer sessions include lighter work from the opposite emphasis;
- scale exercise difficulty and initial volume by starting point without changing the general-fitness goal;
- select weeks 1–4 directly from the reviewer-approved `PrescriptionTrack`. A track may change only one declared dimension between adjacent steps: repetitions, work seconds, sets, tempo enum, or range enum;
- define conservative effort copy as: stop a set when the user estimates they could complete 3 more technically consistent repetitions. The UI explains this as an estimate, not a measured threshold;
- return a typed explanation code and plain-language explanation for every material decision;
- fail closed with a clear unsupported-input error rather than silently inventing content.

Deterministic selection order:

1. Validate `EligiblePlannerInput`, catalog version, and policy version.
2. Create calendar slots from `availableDays` and alternate A/B chronologically.
3. For each session, iterate the policy's ordered emphasis families.
4. Filter catalog items by family, starting-point compatibility, equipment, and adjacency constraints.
5. Select the lowest `selectionPriority`, breaking ties by stable exercise ID, only when all four reviewed prescription steps for that exercise fit their applicable weekly budget.
6. Lock the selected exercise identity for all four weeks. If its complete track does not fit, reject the candidate as a unit and try the next compatible exercise; never substitute only a later week.
7. Stop when no candidate fits. A valid session requires the policy's minimum family coverage; failure returns `PlanGenerationError` rather than a partial plan.

If no compatible exercise has a complete four-week track that fits, the content/policy combination is invalid and CI must fail. Runtime does not downgrade or modify reviewed progression.

### Trust and source policy

- General guidance cites primary public-health or professional-body sources.
- Exercise demonstrations are selected manually from official health-system, public-health, or credentialed professional-organization channels. Search ranking, popularity, and creator claims are not sufficient.
- Store direct video links as reviewed content. Do not search YouTube dynamically and do not substitute a broken link automatically.
- Display publisher and last-reviewed date near the link. Open links in a new tab with clear external-site labeling.
- Run a scheduled link check, but require human review before replacing content because a reachable video can still change or become unsuitable.
- Illustrations must be original or licensed for repository and product use; attribution requirements live with the asset metadata.

## Public Interfaces and Data Shapes

- `PlannerPreferences`: commitment and starting-point answers from the UI; this is the only user state eligible for browser persistence.
- `StoredPreferences`: versioned `localStorage` envelope for `PlannerPreferences`.
- `ExerciseDefinition`: the reviewed content record, including source provenance and progression relationships.
- `ProgramPolicy`: versioned duration, frequency, starting-point, recovery, and progression rules.
- `GeneratedPlan`: policy version, four weekly schedules, session definitions, exercise prescriptions, safety copy version, and explanation entries.
- `ExplanationEntry`: stable code, user-facing message, and the input or rule that caused it.

These are internal TypeScript contracts in v1, not network APIs. Keep them as simple serializable fixtures; do not build adapters, migrations, or remote-content infrastructure for the deferred knowledge base.

```ts
type Weekday = "mon" | "tue" | "wed" | "thu" | "fri" | "sat" | "sun";
type StartingPoint = "new" | "lightly_active" | "returning";
type Family = "knee" | "posterior_hip" | "push" | "upper_back" | "trunk" | "conditioning";
type SetupRequirement = "floor" | "wall" | "stable_chair";
type PrescriptionTrack = readonly [PrescriptionStep, PrescriptionStep, PrescriptionStep, PrescriptionStep];

interface PlannerPreferences {
  minutesPerSession: 15 | 30 | 45;
  availableDays: readonly [Weekday, Weekday] |
    readonly [Weekday, Weekday, Weekday] |
    readonly [Weekday, Weekday, Weekday, Weekday];
  startingPoint: StartingPoint;
}

interface StoredPreferences {
  schemaVersion: 1;
  preferences: PlannerPreferences;
}

type PrescriptionStepBase = {
  sets: number;
  tempo: "natural" | "controlled" | "three_second_lower";
  range: "reduced" | "comfortable" | "full_comfortable";
  restSeconds: number;
  changedDimension: "baseline" | "reps" | "work" | "sets" | "tempo" | "range";
};

type PrescriptionStep = PrescriptionStepBase & (
  | { mode: "reps"; reps: number }
  | { mode: "timed"; workSeconds: number }
);

interface SourceRecord {
  demonstrationUrl: string;
  publisher: string;
  evidenceUrl: string;
  reviewedBy: string;
  lastCheckedAt: string;
}

interface ExerciseDefinition {
  id: string;
  name: string;
  family: Family;
  difficulty: 1 | 2 | 3;
  compatibleStartingPoints: readonly StartingPoint[];
  setup: readonly SetupRequirement[];
  selectionPriority: number;
  secondsPerRep?: number;
  easierExerciseId: string | null;
  harderExerciseId: string | null;
  terminalEasierModification?: string;
  tracks: Partial<Record<StartingPoint, PrescriptionTrack>>;
  illustrationPath: string;
  illustrationAlt: string;
  cues: readonly string[];
  stopGuidance: string;
  educationalRationale: string;
  source: SourceRecord;
}

interface ProgramPolicy {
  version: string;
  catalogVersion: string;
  durationBudgets: Record<15 | 30 | 45, {
    warmupSeconds: number;
    mainSeconds: number;
    cooldownSeconds: number;
    bufferSeconds: number;
  }>;
  requiredFamilies: Record<15 | 30 | 45, { A: readonly Family[]; B: readonly Family[] }>;
  transitionSeconds: 15;
}

interface ExplanationEntry {
  code: "schedule.selected_days" | "schedule.adjacent_days" | "duration.allocation" |
    "level.starting_point" | "exercise.selection" | "progression.week_step";
  message: string;
  causedBy: string;
}

interface GeneratedSession {
  label: "A" | "B";
  weekday: Weekday;
  warmupSequenceId: string;
  cooldownSequenceId: string;
  estimatedDurationSeconds: number;
  exercises: readonly { exerciseId: string; prescription: PrescriptionStep }[];
}

interface GeneratedWeek {
  weekNumber: 1 | 2 | 3 | 4;
  sessions: readonly GeneratedSession[];
}

interface GeneratedPlan {
  policyVersion: string;
  catalogVersion: string;
  safetyCopyVersion: string;
  estimatedMinutesPerSession: 15 | 30 | 45;
  weeks: readonly [GeneratedWeek, GeneratedWeek, GeneratedWeek, GeneratedWeek];
  explanations: readonly ExplanationEntry[];
}

type PlanGenerationResult =
  | { status: "ok"; plan: GeneratedPlan }
  | { status: "error"; code: "invalid_input" | "invalid_content" | "no_valid_plan" };
```

Progression edges point from easier to harder and must be acyclic. Easier/harder references are nullable at terminal exercises; they are not required reciprocal pointers.

Content validation requires exactly one track for every declared compatible starting point and forbids tracks for undeclared starting points. A rep-based definition must include `secondsPerRep`; a timed definition does not use it.

Every successful plan must contain all mandatory explanation categories: selected-day schedule, adjacent-day limitation when present, duration allocation, starting-level choice, one explanation per selected exercise, and one explanation per week-to-week prescription step.

## Interaction and Failure States

| State | Required behavior |
|---|---|
| Normal | Intake, plan summary, explanation, exercise detail, edit-input action, and print action follow the approved wireframe. |
| Disclaimer not acknowledged | Keep preferences, focus the acknowledgment, and do not call the compiler. |
| Invalid/incomplete intake | Keep preferences, focus the first invalid field, provide inline text errors, and disable generation until complete. |
| Direct result navigation or refresh | Restore valid saved preferences, require a fresh acknowledgment, and do not regenerate automatically. |
| Compiler/content failure | Show a generic failure, preserve non-health commitment answers in memory for retry, and expose no partial plan. |
| Missing illustration | Render meaningful alt text and written cues; CI treats a missing required asset as a release failure. |
| Broken external video | Keep the exercise and written cues, label the demonstration unavailable, and raise an operations alert. |
| Mobile | Present the same three stages vertically with no horizontal scrolling at 320 CSS pixels. |
| Print | Produce a calendar page followed by A/B session instructions, abbreviated cues, and a numbered source list; target 4–6 Letter/A4 pages. |

## Success Criteria

- A first-time user can pass intake and reach a plan in under three minutes without creating an account.
- All 819 duration × starting-point × valid-weekday-set cases generate deterministically and stay within the estimated session budget including buffer.
- Every generated session includes a warm-up, policy-required emphasis coverage, a cool-down, and at least one explanation tied to commitment or starting point; weekly output satisfies full movement-family coverage.
- Every referenced exercise has an illustration, an easier exercise or terminal same-exercise modification, concise cues, stop guidance, reviewed demonstration, publisher, evidence/source link, reviewer, and last-checked date.
- The compiler is never called until the safety disclaimer has been acknowledged, and no health answer is collected or stored.
- The same planner input, policy version, and catalog version always produce the same plan and explanation trace.
- The plan remains readable at mobile width and through browser print/save-to-PDF.
- In a five-person beginner usability check, at least four can identify their first workout, explain why the schedule fits them, and find an easier exercise option without assistance.

## Release-blocking automated test follow-up

Automated tests are explicitly deferred from the first prototype implementation. The prototype CI runs lint, strict type checking, content validation, and the production build. Public launch remains blocked until the complete suite below is implemented and passing; the detailed inventory is tracked in `TODOS.md`.

- **Compiler matrix:** exhaustively run all 91 valid weekday subsets across 3 durations and 3 starting points, for 819 total cases. Apply invariant/property assertions to every case and keep human-reviewed snapshots for representative adjacent and non-adjacent patterns.
- **Invariants:** assert supported exercises only, valid catalog references, no over-budget sessions, required warm-up/cool-down, permitted frequency patterns, safe substitutions, and exactly one planned progression dimension at a time.
- **Safety acknowledgment:** verify generation is blocked until acknowledgment, no health answers are collected, and acknowledgment never persists across reloads.
- **Explanation trace:** verify every schedule, level, and progression decision has a stable explanation code and readable copy.
- **Content integrity:** validate schema, unique IDs, non-circular progression links, illustration existence, provenance fields, and unused catalog entries.
- **External sources:** scheduled URL reachability report with manual approval for replacements; broken links degrade to written cues rather than hiding the exercise.
- **UI:** meet WCAG 2.2 AA; test keyboard-only flow, screen-reader labels, focus and error behavior, narrow mobile layout, print stylesheet, contrast, target size, and reduced-motion behavior. Any automated or manual finding that violates an applicable AA success criterion blocks release.
- **Privacy:** verify the app sends no intake answers to a backend, analytics service, or third-party video provider before the user opens an external link.

## Distribution Plan

- Intended stack: TanStack Start, React 19, strict TypeScript, Vite static prerendering, and Netlify's official TanStack Start integration. Personalized computation remains client-side with no server functions.
- Keep v1 stateless and browser-only. Prefer a static or prerendered application with no database and no application API.
- Use Netlify preview deployments for pull requests and a production deployment from the protected main branch.
- Prototype CI runs lint, type checks, content validation, and a production build. Before public launch, CI must also run unit tests, the full compiler matrix, accessibility checks, and browser coverage recorded in `TODOS.md`.
- Run source-link checking weekly. Alert on unreachable URLs and on records older than 180 days since `lastCheckedAt`; never modify reviewed content automatically.

## Release Gates and Ownership

- **Product owner:** owns scope, copy integration, source operations, and the final release decision.
- **Qualified fitness reviewer:** must approve the supported-user boundary, safety disclaimer and acknowledgment copy, seed exercise catalog, every prescription track, substitutions, stop guidance, duration assumptions, and progression policy. Approval is recorded with reviewer identity, credentials, date, and content versions.
- **Engineering owner:** must demonstrate passing CI, the complete generated-plan matrix, no missing assets, and no unresolved violation of an applicable WCAG 2.2 AA success criterion.
- Public launch is blocked until all three roles sign off. A preview may be used for supervised usability sessions before fitness review, but must be labeled “prototype - do not follow as an exercise prescription.”
